[113] Heading 2: 3.4 Selection of Wildfire Conditioning Factors
[114] normal: The selection of conditioning factors was guided by the literature review and the specific topographic and climatic realities of SGNP. Five primary factors were selected:
[115] normal: 1. Slope: Influences the rate of fire spread. Fires move faster uphill due to pre-heating of adjacent vegetation.
[116] normal: 2. Elevation: Affects microclimate, specifically temperature and humidity variations across the park.
[117] normal: 3. Vegetation Cover (NDVI): Represents fuel load. Denser, drier vegetation significantly increases ignition potential.
[118] normal: 4. Proximity to Roads: Acts as a proxy for human activity. In urban parks, fires are frequently ignited near access routes due to discarded cigarettes or campfires.
[119] normal: 5. Proximity to Settlements: Represents the urban edge effect, where human-wildlife conflict and encroachment increase fire probability.
